Skippers' page: Guidance to SDC skippers

Goals

Goals of the program include:

  • Helping skippers get more value from their boats. Some skippers may use their boats more if experienced sailors are available to help them manage their boat. This is particularly true for new skippers. Skippers may also use their boats more frequently if they have made a commitment to participate on specific days through the season.
  • Building a supportive community of cruisers.
  • Helping sailors develop the confidence and competence needed to command their own boat.
  • Giving skippers and sailors the chance to test-drive a range of boat models and get exposure to a range of skipper styles.
  • Introducing young people to the pastime.
  • Increasing the value of club membership for those who don't own a boat.

On-water communications

Skippers are encouraged to monitor VHF channel 68. However, VHF is not reliable over the full length of the lake. Text or voice on the mobile network is an alternative.

Use the what3words app for communicating location.

Weather

A cruise will be abandoned if there is a Government of Canada thunderstorm warning in effect for Ottawa one hour before start.

In addition, a cruise will be abandoned if the Government of Canada forecast Air Quality Health Index for Ottawa is 8 or higher.

The decision to sail rests solely with the skipper. If a Government of Canada thunderstorm watch (as opposed to a warning) is in effect for Ottawa, skippers may choose to sail in the vicinity of the harbour and monitor weather radar.

If a skipper becomes aware of approaching storm cells, they should communicate this fact to other boats.

Sailors may, independently, choose not to venture out - their prerogative.

We don't abandon due to light winds. However, cruises are commonly cut short if winds are too light.

Assistance on the water

In the event a boat requires assistance, other members of the group can be contacted by VHF or mobile. Some skippers are qualified to use the club's chase boats. However, these must not be used for towing.

In the case of a threat to life or serious personal injury, call 911.

In less serious situations, the Ottawa Fire Service can be contacted at (613) 232-1551.

The club does not offer on-water assistance.

Assignments

Crews are picked using a neural nework. Consequently, we are not able to accommodate requests to sail on a specific boat or with specific crew members.

Where possible, we limit the number of sailors to the minimum for each boat. This is intended to maximize the opportunity for sailors to practice their skills.

We rotate sailors through the fleet in the course of the season. This gives them exposure to multiple boat types and skippers' styles.

We attempt to match the skill levels of sailors in each crew. This makes for a more satisfactory experience for all. Skippers may request that at least one member of the crew be an experienced sailor.

Of course, a skipper may invite friends and family to join them on their boat during a Social Day-cruising event. Such arrangements are outside the scope of the program. And in these cases, it is helpful if the individuals in question do NOT register to sail in the program. Otherwise, they may get assigned to a different boat.

Registrations and cancellations may occur up to 10:00 AM on the day of a cruise. Consequently, specific crew assignments will be decided close to the time of the cruise. However, late cancellation is strongly discouraged.

Over capacity

Early in the season, we may experience excess capacity. Boats that have sailed in the program the fewest number of times will be given priority in this circumstance.

Skippers of boats that are available, but not required, have choices: take a rest, participate with their own crew or act as a sailor on one of the boats that is sailing.

Apres sailing

Cruisers are invited to gather after the cruise on the club deck. We try to sit close to one another. Therefore, the first sailor to return is asked to take a table in the shade at the top of the outdoor club stairs. The club manager has been asked to configure seating appropriately.

The intention is NOT to exclude non-cruisers, but merely to place cruisers in close proximity.

How to get involved

Getting involved is a two-step process using a self-service facility that is accessible from the program page.

First of all, the boat owner must enrol in the program. Then they must register for specific cruise dates.

Both of these steps may be repeated if circumstances change during the season. For instance, the maximum capacity may be adjusted if the owner invites their own guests.
